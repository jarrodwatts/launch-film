# Story

How a 30 to 40s launch film is shaped, why, and how to write the options the user picks from.
Evidence tags as in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md): PROVEN, PROVEN BY REJECTION, RESEARCH, THEORY.

---

## 1. The spine, part by part

```
 HOOK      BUILD (voices stack, faster)     TENSION       DROP = REVEAL            POINT     BREADTH    LOOP       BREAK       RE-DROP
 one       more, faster, wall, headline     one question  input -> work -> output  said      every      why it     hero alone  logo, line,
 voice                                      in near-quiet -> one tap -> done       plainly   option     spreads    under lamp  CTA
 |----------------- music: intro / energy / quiet ------|---------------------- drop --------------------------|--break--|-redrop-|
```

This is the user's own brief from the Auni v1 round, which the film then proved: "think harder
about how a viral video might kind of like build up momentum, stack takes up on top of eachother,
focus on building up tension, and then reach a climax where the beat migth drop and then we reveal
what auni does in an obvious manner." PROVEN.

| Part | Its job | Auni | Share of film |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | Stop the scroll on frame 1 with something known and a bit spicy | The founder's real X post types out "Nvidia is going to $10 trillion." | 25% with build |
| Build | Stack voices, faster and faster, until it is a wall | Counter-takes slam in 0.5s then 0.17s apart; camera pulls back on an endless wall; "Everyone has a take." | |
| Tension | One line, alone, in the song's quiet bar. It calls the viewer out | @auni_io asks "ok but are you positioned?" | 7% |
| Drop = reveal | The product answers the question on the drop: input, the work, the output, one tap, done | The take lands in Auni, research steps, "Found 4 moves", the card, the tap, "All 4 moves placed" | 23% |
| Point | Say what it is, plainly, once | "Your take. Now a position." | 7% |
| Breadth | Everything else it can do, as one fast accumulating montage | Venues, markets, a galaxy, a roller landing on "Every market.", "One chat." | 15% |
| Loop | Why it spreads, as a scene that answers the film's own question | You reply "I am." with the position; real people copy; coins fly back | 18% |
| Break | The hero alone, big and centred, on the song's break | The 3D mascot turns to camera under its own lamp | 13% with re-drop |
| Re-drop | The logo slams on the re-drop, the line lands, the real CTA holds | Lockup, "Put your money behind what you believe.", "Join the waitlist with X" | |

## 2. Structure lessons

- **The drop is the reveal.** Every strong teardown locks its biggest visual payoff to the music's
  biggest change: film A about 2s, film C 5s, film G 6.8s, film B 8.2s, film E 9.0s, film F 10.0s.
  Place the drop at 5 to 10s. Auni's is at 9.7s, after a longer build the user asked for.
  RESEARCH, then PROVEN.
- **The product asks the question the drop answers.** "ok but are you positioned?" was first posted
  by "anon". The user: "why is it from anon? i don't really think that makes sense. maybe it should
  be frmo me? or from auni? wdyt?". Moved to the product's own account, it set up the drop and later
  the social loop (you reply "I am."). Every speaker needs a reason to be there. PROVEN.
- **One hero input runs the whole film.** The same take is typed, sent, researched, turned into a
  card, flipped, posted and copied. Nothing new has to be explained mid-film. PROVEN.
- **Say the point once, in plain words, right after the reveal.** "Your take. Now a position." Films
  A and H never say what they are, and their view rates suffer. PROVEN in the film; RESEARCH on
  the cost.
- **Show the thinking, and hold the aha.** The user: "maybe pause for a little longer on 'found X
  moves' so people kinda get ohh okay it converted the input into a portfolio of outputs!". The
  found step then physically grew into the card's header. PROVEN.
- **Bridge each new topic from what the viewer already knows.** The user on the social section:
  "think about what context a viewer has so far, and now we're kind introducing a completely new
  topic to them". A "Public on Discover" toggle and a leaderboard both failed. Answering the film's
  own question on X ("I am.") worked. PROVEN BY REJECTION.
- **One complete flow, then breadth in one fast montage.** Film F spends 15s on three near-identical
  forms. Auni does one flow, then a galaxy and a roller. RESEARCH and PROVEN.
- **Breadth accumulates.** "build up slowly the offerings pile up and it gets more and more and then
  maybe kinda like broad scope out". A partner is featured by order and small labels (first in the
  pile, "xStocks on Monad"), never by size: "subtely ... dont be too obnoxious about it". PROVEN.
- **Re-climb in the second half so the middle never sags.** Film A has two peaks; the reference studio's
  reel has a breakdown, then its loudest hit. Auni: the loop accelerates into a coin pour, then the
  break, then the re-drop. PROVEN.
- **The back half is the pitch, not a fade-out.** "29 seconds and beyond is lackluster IMO. you
  haven't really told the proper sales pitch", "the remaining last ~15 seconds just feels
  incredibly lazy". Effort drops off in the back half of every draft. The user noticed every time.
  PROVEN BY REJECTION.
- **Every feature arrives as the next story step, not as a list.** Film G (the best organic numbers
  in the set) vs film B (seven claims, no story, forgettable). RESEARCH.
- **Show the mechanism, not the numbers.** "show the mechanism but not the specific numbers or
  amounts". The social pitch had no amounts anywhere. PROVEN.
- **A payoff proves the result.** "really solidify hey yeah the positions were placed". The good
  version is literal ("All 4 moves placed", holdings burst across the frame), not a sticker.
  PROVEN BY REJECTION.

## 3. Hooks: the first 1 to 3 seconds

Only about 1 in 4 people who see a post count as a 2-second view, so frame 1 and the post text carry
most of the message. RESEARCH.

Hooks that work (RESEARCH, from measured launches):

- a headline written out on frame 1, before anything moves;
- a money number as the first readable thing;
- a known thing, transformed;
- a mystery or a question;
- a human scene with the product as an overlay;
- the founder to camera (for "first ever" claims);
- the problem as comedy.

Weakest: a logo, a title card, a disclaimer, a static hold. Film E's 1.75s still opening and film B's
logo-first opening are the cautionary cases.

**Concrete beats abstract.** Auni's first hook, "AI keeps winning.", failed: "not really like
somethign the user can grasp ... its kinda just like a random statement". What worked: a real,
recognisable account (the founder's own: "use my twitetr account @jarrodwatts") typing a funny,
polarising, current take with a money number in it. The background takes were that week's real
arguments. "make the takes a little more controversial on topics that are hot right now. i like
Nvidia is going to 10 trillion thats kinda funny." PROVEN with the user. Reach untested.

**Real formats, at a size you can read.** "shaped like proper tweets with X watermark and stuff but
keep text big and visible". The viewer recognises the format before they read a word. PROVEN.

**Start slow, then accelerate.** "the first tweet should kinda start slowly, like it types out ...
music might be kinda fading in". Typing over a fading-in song, then the build. PROVEN.

## 4. Controversy rules (RESEARCH)

- **Aim the edge at something the audience already distrusts** (finfluencers, casinos, agents that
  went wrong, insider politicians). Never at the buyer's own identity (Bud Light, Duolingo's
  "AI-first" memo) and never at caution itself (FTX's "Don't be like Larry").
- **Never hang the hook's feeling on prices going up.** Crypto.com's "Fortune favors the brave" ran
  the day BTC was $60.6k; a year later $1,000 was about $340. Hang it on behaviour you can show.
- **Moderate beats maximal.** Once discomfort wins, people go quiet (Chen and Berger 2013). Rage
  can't be taken back at the same volume: Artisan's apology post got 8,830 impressions against
  "billions" for the billboards.
- **For a product that holds money, show control as one quick beat** (the tap). Leading with safety
  got the lowest organic engagement in the set (two brokerages' agent launches).
- **Every claim will be tested against what the product does in public.** Robinhood's "We are all
  investors" aired 10 days after it restricted GME buying.

What Auni did: the edge is a jab at talking without acting ("ok but are you positioned?") over a
pile of loud takes. It pokes a behaviour everyone recognises in themselves, not a group. Control
shows up once, as the tap. PROVEN with the user; THEORY for reach.

## 5. The claims check (RESEARCH, not legal advice)

Before writing a line, check it against this list, and re-decide it per product and per region:

- No returns, projections or "guaranteed". No "beats the market", "passive income", "risk-free".
- No performance screenshots of a real person's results.
- No claims about AI the product can't back up ("predicts the market").
- Say what the thing really is: "tokenized", "tracks the price", not "own Nvidia".
- No "savings account", "safer than a bank", "insured" for anything that isn't.
- Real people shown by name and face need their permission (see `launch-film:assets`).
- Restricted features (prediction markets, perps, anything regulated by country): ask the user.
  Auni's answer: "We'll have disclaimers in the post/terms so you don't need to be concerned about
  that in the video".

## 6. Writing 3 or 4 directions

Write them before any building, after the research. Each direction is:

- a name and one line of what the viewer feels;
- the hook line for the first 2 seconds;
- the enemy or tension, and who it polarises;
- a 6 to 10 line beat list on the spine above;
- where it is strongest (a loud feed, the website, a follow-up cut).

Spread them from calm to provocative. Auni's four:

| Direction | Spice | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Money where mouth is: a take turns into a position | Pokes a habit | Recommended, picked |
| What do you believe?: calm, premium, no enemy | Calm | Best as the website film, weakest in a loud feed |
| It asks first: opens on AI agents losing money | Polarising | Most trust risk |
| Put the chart down: a 3am chart-staring skit | Funny | Needs live action |

The ones not picked become follow-up cuts in launch week.
