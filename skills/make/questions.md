# Questions worth asking

The questions that shaped the Auni film, rewritten for any product. Each has its options, the
recommended pick and why, and what the Auni user answered. Read this before the first question box,
and again before each later round.

## How to ask

- Use the question tool. 2 to 4 multiple-choice items per round. The recommended option goes first
  and says "(Recommended)".
- Each option gets one or two sentences of what it means in practice, including its cost.
- Give previews the user can judge in one look: ASCII mock-ups of the visual worlds, a 6 to 10 line
  beat list per direction, audition clips cut to the film's drop.
- Ask only what only the user knows. Anything you can find by reading, running or checking, find.
- Put links and media in your final message, never in text above a question box. The user can't see
  that text. "where ? i cant see them here".
- If the user can't answer yet (they haven't listened), carry on with your default and say so. That
  was the right call for the Auni song.

```
 ROUND 1 (minute 0)      ROUND 2 (after research)     ROUND 3 (before building)     LATER (as they come up)
 audience                direction (4 scripts)        sound source                  legal stance per venue
 spice level             CTA                          visual world (4 mock-ups)     how to show money
 who builds it           live action or not           mascot role                   which real people
                                                      real 3D or not                what a phrase meant
```

## Round 1: the frame

**Audience. "Who is the main person this film has to win over?"**
- Options: the insiders of the category (they've seen 100 launches like it); normal people who
  would use it (bigger, harder cold start); both, insiders first (recommended); a partner's
  community first.
- Why recommend "both, insiders first": the post spreads through insiders, but the story must land
  for anyone in 5 seconds.
- Auni: "Both, CT first". The film used crypto Twitter's formats (X posts, "are you positioned?"),
  simple enough for anyone.

**Spice. "How spicy are you willing to be?"**
- Options: explore all, pick later (recommended: you write 3 or 4 directions from calm to openly
  provocative); premium and calm; provocative (pick a real enemy); tongue-in-cheek.
- Why: the user can't judge spice in the abstract. They can judge four scripts.
- Auni: "Explore all, pick later". They picked the one that pokes a habit the audience laughs at in
  itself (all talk, no position), not one that attacks a group.

**Production. "Who will actually make the final film?"**
- Options: us, with code and AI (recommended when the bar is high and the UI is the hero); hire an
  agency (you write the brief and storyboard); the user edits it themselves (you give a shot list);
  not decided (show cost in time and quality per route).
- Auni: "Us with AI + code ... be ambitious to create a premium, top-studio level product launch
  video". That answer also set the bar for the whole project.

## Round 2: the story

**Direction. "Which direction should the hero film be?"** Four scripted options, each a beat list.
- Auni's four: "Money where mouth is" (a take turns into a position; recommended), "What do you
  believe?" (calm, premium, no enemy; best as the website film), "It asks first" (opens on AI agents
  losing money; most polarising), "Put the chart down" (a 3am skit; needs live action).
- Say which one is best for a loud feed and which is best for a website. The others can become
  follow-up cuts in launch week.
- Auni: picked the recommended one. It became the whole story.

**CTA. "When the film goes out, what can a viewer do?"**
- Options: a waitlist (ranked by referrals, if they want a loop); sign up and use it now; not
  decided (you design an end card that works either way).
- Reuse the product's real button and tagline from its landing page.
- Auni: "Ummm up to you - like right now it's in a waitlist phase". End card: "Join the waitlist
  with X", the site's own button.

**Live action. "Can the film show people, hands, rooms, or only the product and type?"**
- Options: product and type only (fastest, no AI-look risk, no GPU); a few AI-generated shots; real
  filmed footage.
- Auni: product-only. The user found AI video "a little unreliable". The whole film was built in code.

## Round 3: the look and sound

**Sound source. "Where should the sound come from?"** Say plainly that this is the biggest lever on
"premium".
- Options: the user's own SFX pack, or a free curated one (recommended if they have one); a paid
  library (Epidemic, Artlist); generated (ElevenLabs); a hired sound designer.
- Auni: "hmmm surely we can get it free right ?", plus a link to a free creator pack, plus pasted
  agency advice to pick the music first. The user then sent a 58-file pack.

**Visual world. "What world should the film live in?"** Four ASCII mock-ups:

```
 A. brand colour fields      B. the site's own world     C. dark, glossy, glowing    D. light product,
 +------------------+        +------------------+        +------------------+           colour for type
 |##################|        |  .  ~   .   ~    |        |      .  *        |        +--------+--------+
 |##  BIG  WHITE  ##|        |   [card]  (o_o)  |        |   ((  [card]  )) |        | [card] |########|
 |##   TYPE      ###|        | ~    .   [pill]  |        |        *    .    |        |        |# TYPE #|
 +------------------+        +------------------+        +------------------+        +--------+--------+
```
- Recommend the product's own world when its site has one. One world for the whole film.
- Auni: "Just take the branding and theming and design frmo auni ... don't go over the top but just
  make it consistent". The site's soft sky, its headline type, frosted cards.

**Mascot role** (if the brand has a mascot or a hero object).
- Options: the star (recommended: it drives transitions, its shape becomes wipes, it lands in the
  logo); supporting; logo only.
- Auni: "whatever you think is best". It became the star and the transition device, and the hero of
  the close.

**Real 3D. "Can the film use real 3D?"**
- Options: yes, a 3D mascot and objects (Blender from the brand's SVG; the biggest jump toward
  agency level); 2.5D only; AI image-to-3D first (quicker, may come out off-model).
- Auni: "Yes, a 3D mascot + objects". The 2D fake that came first was rejected: "it's not 3d at
  all".

## Later rounds: as each question comes up

**Legal stance on restricted features.** For Auni: prediction markets and perps are restricted in
some countries. Options: show them in the breadth montage with a small caveat line (recommended);
show them fully; logos only.
- Auni: "We'll have disclaimers in the post/terms so you don't need to be concerned about that in the
  video". Re-ask this per product and per region. Never assume it.

**How to show money or results.** Options: show the mechanism, no figures (recommended); show a
number (punchier, but a made-up result in an ad reads as a performance claim); skip it.
- Auni: "show the mechanism but not the specific numbers or amounts and stuff etc you feel me?".
  No figures anywhere in the social pitch.

**Which real people.** First clarify what the user meant. "tap into famous CT personalities" could
mean real accounts, meme sounds or parody characters. Ask with those three options.
- Auni: "Real accounts in the social bit", then "monad team + angel investors would be cool".
- Then get the exact handles. Give lanes that exact list and nothing more (see
  `launch-film:assets`). Every real person goes on the before-you-post list.

**The one line.** When a section needs a pitch line, offer 2 or 3 written out.
- Auni: "Get paid when people copy you (Recommended)" was picked and closed the social section.

**Music.** Not a question box until the user can listen. Send audition links in the final message,
each starting about 9s before the drop. Then ask "which track, after listening?". See
`launch-film:sound`.

## What made the good questions good

- **A recommended option and a concrete preview.** The user mostly took the recommendation, or
  answered in free text with a sharper idea.
- **Things only the user knows:** audience, risk appetite, budget, brand, who may appear, legal
  stance, what a phrase meant.
- **Clarifying the user's own words.** Asking what "famous CT personalities" meant saved a whole
  wrong build.

## Questions that wasted a round

- **Infrastructure.** Asked twice where a GPU box was. Answers: "figure it out", then a pointer to a
  GPU machine you can SSH into. Search SSH config, the VPN's device list, shell history and memory yourself. Find
  it once and remember it.
- **Media the user couldn't see.** Song links written above a question box. Put them in the final
  message.
- **Asking for a judgement before the user could make it.** "Which track, after listening?" before
  they had listened. Go ahead with a default and keep it swappable.
- **Paid tools before free ones.** The user's first answer to "ElevenLabs or a library?" was "surely
  we can get it free right ?". Offer the free route first when one exists.
